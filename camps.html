Elite Offensive Skills

A real jump, in one week.

Consecutive days on one skill set — the fastest a player improves all year.

See Camp Dates

You've probably paid for a camp that was daycare with pucks.

Big groups. A coach running the same station for four days. Lots of movement, lots of tired kids, and nothing a parent could point to at the end of the week and say that's different now.

The week isn't the problem. A week is enough time to genuinely change a skill. What's missing is a plan for what to change, and someone watching closely enough to change it.

Consecutive days are the whole point.

Our Method runs a skill through eight steps — from a standing drill to a live game where the read is forced. In a once-a-week lesson, that progression takes a month or more, and every session starts with re-warming ground the player has half-forgotten.

In a camp, it doesn't. Day one builds the tool. Day two picks up exactly where day one stopped, while it's still fresh. By the end of the week the same skill has been through resistance, a live defender, and a game — and it's still there.

Same number of hours as five lessons across five weeks. A completely different result.

Every discipline, in one block.

Camps are where the whole system runs at once. Every day mixes on-ice and off-ice, because they do different jobs and a player needs both.

On the ice

Where a skill goes to full game speed, against real defenders, with real reads.

The shooting lab

Mechanics, volume and video review, with the coach close enough to correct every rep.

Off-ice

Speed and strength work that makes the on-ice work possible.

Games

Small-area games where the day's skill has to hold up under pressure.

16 players · 2–3 coaches

That ratio is the constraint everything else is built around — it's what makes it possible to actually watch a player's reps and correct them, which is the entire difference between a camp that works and a camp that doesn't.

Three different products, not one camp on repeat.

Winter and spring break — fix what's costing them right now

Mid-season, short and focused: one or two fixes rather than a rebuild. Spring break lands right before spring hockey and tryout season.

Summer — 5 Week Elite, the real rebuild

Five weeks is long enough to take a skill apart and put it back together, and long enough for the off-ice work to actually change what a player's body can do. The biggest jumps of the year come out of this block.

August — prep camps

Two to three weeks before the season starts. Sharpening, not rebuilding — a player walks into September tryouts at game speed instead of finding it in October.

U9 to U18 — and the players who've already moved past it.

Camps run from U9 through U18, grouped by age and by level so a player is always training with people who push them.

We also run players who are past minor hockey: junior, WHL and college athletes come back to train here in the off-season. Same Method, same standards — the progression doesn't change at the top, the starting point does.

A week isn't a program.

A camp is the fastest week of the year, not a substitute for the rest of it. What a good camp does is put a real skill in a player's hands quickly — and give them something worth building on.

Players who come out of a camp and keep training hold the gain. Players who don't, don't. We'd rather say that up front than pretend a week is enough.

They come home with something they didn't have on Monday.

Not tired. Better. A move that works against a real defender, a shot that goes where they aimed it, and the experience of what it feels like to actually improve at something — which is the thing that makes a player want to keep going.
