Our Story

I built the program I wish I'd had.

Twenty years, one question: how does a player get better as fast as humanly possible — the right way?

I got good the hard way.

I was a house-league player at 13. At 18 I was having conversations with NHL scouts.

I got there by grinding — years of skills work, mostly on my own, whatever I could figure out. And it worked. But it took far longer than it should have, because I never had a real plan, and I never had a mentor who truly knew what they were doing.

I wasted years on things that didn't matter. Nobody told me which things did. I just trained harder, and hoped harder was the same as better.

Then the training I trusted broke me.

The strength training I did at a hockey-specific gym left me with chronic back problems and shoulders that needed multiple surgeries.

Improper training ultimately cost me the shot I'd worked my whole life for.

That's the part I can't let go of. Not that I didn't make it — plenty of players don't. It's that what stopped me wasn't a lack of work or a lack of want. It was training that nobody was checking. It was making me tired, and we all assumed that was the same thing as making me better.

There's no neutral in training. Every session makes a player better or worse.

So I've spent twenty years on one question.

How does a player get better, as fast as possible, the right way?

I went at it the way I wish someone had gone at it for me. Thousands of hours of film, watching what the best players in the world actually do — not what coaching orthodoxy says they do. Breaking elite skills into pieces small enough to teach. Working out which pieces actually matter and throwing away the rest. Then designing drills that force a skill out of a drill and into a game, because a skill that only works in a drill isn't a skill.

Twenty years of that became the Lightsteel Method. Eight steps, run the same way every time, through every discipline we teach.

How We Train

The point was never to be the only one who could do it.

Coaching at this level takes a rare combination — a real hockey eye from playing the game, thousands of hours of film, the ability to break skills down, and the ability to build drills that transfer. Almost nobody has all four. I got there by spending twenty years on it.

You can't build a business hoping to find more people like that. So I didn't. I built the expertise into the system instead, and I train high-level players to run it.

That means a player doesn't get a different program depending on which coach they draw. They get the same Method, the same language, the same standard — every session.
